Our Story

From a side project to 4,000 teams

LinkFlow started as a weekend experiment after our founders spent a full week manually syncing Salesforce with a homegrown data warehouse. There had to be a better way.

2021 — London
Rift spotted
Arjun and Fiona quit their jobs after a shared epiphany: every ops team they knew was drowning in the same disconnected-tool problem.
2022 — Seed round
First 100 customers & $4M raised
Word spread fast. We launched to a waitlist of 600, onboarded our first 100 teams in 6 weeks, and closed a seed round to fund the core platform.
2023 — FluxEngine
AI layer ships
We launched FluxEngine — the AI decision layer that reads intent, not just events. Customer NPS jumped 28 points in the quarter it launched.
2025 — Series B
$42M to close the rift for every enterprise
We raised our Series B to build deeper enterprise infrastructure, expand to APAC and LATAM, and grow to 340+ integrations.
What We Believe

The principles that shape everything

Ruthless reliability

Workflows are mission-critical infrastructure. We hold ourselves to 99.99% uptime and treat every downtime minute as a personal failure. Reliability isn't a feature — it's the foundation.

People over pipelines

Technology should amplify human judgement, not replace it. We build tools that give people back the time to think, create, and make decisions — not tools that make decisions for them.

Radical transparency

Every workflow run is fully observable. Every pricing tier is honestly documented. We don't hide complexity behind abstraction — we make it legible so you stay in control.

Default to speed

Slow iteration is a form of disrespect to the people waiting for a solution. We ship fast, learn fast, and fix fast. Perfection is a direction, not a destination before launch.

Security is non-negotiable

We handle sensitive data for thousands of teams. SOC 2 Type II, end-to-end encryption, and zero-trust architecture aren't checkboxes — they're the baseline we hold ourselves to from day one.

Genuine curiosity

The best automation ideas don't come from engineers alone — they come from listening obsessively to the people running operations at 2am. We stay close to the problem, always.
